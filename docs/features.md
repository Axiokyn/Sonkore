# Features

[← Back](../README.md)

| Area | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Formats** | FLAC (up to 24 bit / 384 kHz and beyond; FLAC 1.4's 32-bit files through FFmpeg), WAV (PCM and float) and MP3 with dr_libs (decoded to float, never through 16 bits). With FFmpeg (bundled in the AppImage and the Windows package; loaded only when needed): ALAC and AAC (`.m4a`), Opus, Vorbis, FLAC in Ogg, AIFF, W64, APE, WavPack, TTA, TAK, Musepack, DSD (`.dsf`/`.dff`, compressed DST too), WMA, MKA and more. Integer lossless formats (ALAC, AIFF, APE, WavPack, TTA) reach the DAC unconverted, like FLAC, so they keep the bit-perfect path; in MP4 the AAC encoder padding is trimmed |
| **Library** | Watched folders with recursive, incremental scanning (inotify), a JSON cache, FLAC/ID3v2/RIFF tags (and MP4/Vorbis/APE/ASF with FFmpeg), embedded covers or `cover.jpg`, and **missing covers from the Cover Art Archive** (the album is looked up on MusicBrainz by artist and album; the picture stays in the cache, never in your folders). Off by default: turn it on in Settings › Library. Track and album views, and the **folder tree in the sidebar**. The queue, the equalizer and the settings are on the playback bar |
| **Columns** | 20 columns (by default file name, album, format and time; any can be removed; the first one takes the free space; title, artist, album, genre, year, track, disc, time, format, codec, sample rate, bits, channels, bitrate, size, file, folder, date added, modified…): shown or hidden with a right click on the header, **moved by dragging the header**, resized by dragging the divider (which follows the pointer; a double click restores the width), and sorted by clicking the header |
| **Selection and drag and drop** | Multiple selection with Ctrl / Shift / Ctrl+A. Tracks are dragged onto a playlist in the sidebar, the queue or "new playlist". Files or folders can be dropped from the file manager onto a playlist, and a playlist is reordered by dragging |
| **Gapless** | The next track is opened ahead of time and spliced without silence, sample-exact and without reopening the device, also with the equalizer, pitch or volume in use (verified bit for bit). Encoder padding is trimmed (LAME in MP3, AAC in MP4, Opus pre-skip). If the sample rate or channels change, the DAC is reopened in the new format. The end of the last track is never cut off: the device stops only after it has played its buffer |
| **ReplayGain** | Per-track or per-album leveling (ReplayGain 2.0, −18 LUFS reference), with preamp and *true-peak* clipping prevention (the peak stays at −1 dBTP, the EBU R128 ceiling, because a 4× meter can read up to ~0.6 dB low). It reads `REPLAYGAIN_*` tags (FLAC, Ogg, APE, WavPack, MP4, ID3 `TXXX`) and `R128_*` (Opus); untagged tracks are measured in the background with libebur128 (EBU R128), album by album, with mono weighted as it is heard (on both speakers), 7.1 side channels counted and the *true peak* of lossy files measured above 0 dBFS. Files are never modified: measured values stay in the library cache. Off by default; the gain changes at the exact sample of a gapless join |
| **Playlists** | Create, rename, delete, add from any view, import/export M3U/M3U8. A queue with shuffle and repeat (all/one) that is reordered by dragging and restored on reopening. A click on the title in the playback bar takes the open list to the playing song (or opens its folder) |
| **Mini player** | `Ctrl+M`: the window turns into a small player (Winamp style) with its own list below: the queue or a library folder, with its own columns and order. A search box that filters as you type (`Ctrl+F`; `Enter` plays the first result) and a button that takes the list to the playing song (switching to its folder if needed). When the first library folder is added, the list shows it on its own |
| **Settings per output** | Each output device remembers its equalizer, convolution, crossfeed, headroom, dither, oversampling and DSD settings: headphones and speakers each come back with their own when you switch outputs (Settings › Audio; a new output starts from the current settings) |
| **Equalizer** | 10-band graphic or parametric with up to 32 bands (peak, shelves, LP/HP, BP, notch), an interactive response curve (drag points in frequency and gain without crossing into neighbouring bands, wheel = Q, double click = standard frequency in graphic mode), preamp with a clipping warning and an "Auto" mode, 13 presets and your own profiles, import/export of parametric profiles (`.txt`, including Equalizer APO's slope-based shelves). Peak and shelf bands use a **matched design** (Vicanek) instead of the bilinear transform: the curve does not collapse near Nyquist (a +6 dB band at 16 kHz at 44.1 kHz gives +6.0 dB, previously +2.1) and sounds the same at 44.1, 96 or 192 kHz. In graphic mode the gains are solved together so the curve passes through each slider's value (one-octave bands overlap: ten at +6 dB used to give +8.8 dB). Turning the equalizer on, off or flat is a ~10 ms fade, with no jumps. Optional **linear phase**: the same curve as an FIR filter (within 0.01 dB of the biquads; from 0.5 s to 4 s of taps depending on how much the narrowest band rings, so a Q 10 notch in the bass reaches its full depth), which delays every frequency equally and leaves transient timing and the stereo image intact; it switches on, changes and switches off during playback without dropouts or repeats, also across gapless tracks |
| **AutoEQ** | Searches the ~7,000 headphones in [AutoEq](https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/AutoEq), applies the parametric correction and saves it as a profile |
| **Output** | Devices listed by backend: on Linux PipeWire first, **spoken natively** (with "System default"): PipeWire's graph follows each track's sample rate while Sonkore plays (*Follow the track's rate*, handed back after a minute paused), the stream volume stays at 100 %, and the signal path warns if PipeWire converts the rate or a software volume drops below 100 %; verified by recording the DAC: identical to the file up to 24 bits. Then the cards as direct ALSA `hw:` (exclusive, bit-perfect) and finally JACK. On Windows, WASAPI (shared or exclusive) and the installed **ASIO** drivers (direct, bit-perfect, in the driver's own sample format: 16/24/32-bit integer or float). Switching devices happens live and keeps the position; a DAC that is plugged in or out is detected and reported |
| **DSD** | Settings › Audio › DSD playback: *Convert to PCM* (the default, any DAC; with an **in-house double-precision converter**: a 160 dB FIR evaluated through byte tables up to 352.8 kHz and then SoX VHQ, DSD64 → 88.2 kHz flat to 30 kHz, DSD128 and up → 176.4 kHz flat to 50–70 kHz; time-aligned with the source and with DSD's ultrasonic noise filtered: ~−120 dBFS above the band versus ~−28 dBFS with FFmpeg's converter), *Native* (Linux: DSD_U32_BE/LE through ALSA to DACs that offer it on an `hw:` output; Windows: ASIO 2.2's DSD mode on drivers that have it; as high as the DAC goes) or *DoP* (DSD over PCM on a direct, exclusive or ASIO output). DSF and DSDIFF, and **DST** too (compressed DSDIFF, the SACD format): an in-house DST decoder (ISO/IEC 14496-3, subpart 10) returns the exact bits frame by frame, so it also plays as native DSD or DoP; the bitstream arrives intact (verified byte by byte), with DSD silence (`0x69`) instead of zeros, a few ms of pre-roll so the DAC can switch modes, DoP markers always alternating, and gapless playback between DSD tracks of the same rate. DSP and digital volume do not apply to DSD; hardware volume does. If the output cannot carry it intact, it is converted to PCM |
| **Signal path** | The status pill in the playback bar ("Bit-perfect · 96 kHz", "Resampled · 48 kHz", "Processed · 44.1 kHz") opens it, and the **Now playing** page (click the cover in the bar) shows it next to the large cover and the queue: every stage from the file to the DAC (source, decoder, memory, HDCD, DSD, pitch/tempo, equalizer, ReplayGain, volume, resampling, requantization, output and the audio thread's priority) with its quality, as in Roon: *Lossless*, *High quality*, *Enhanced* (chosen DSP) or *Low quality* |
| **Playback from memory** | Settings › Audio › Play from memory: each file (up to 2 GB) is read into RAM before it plays and, if the system allows it (`RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`), locked there; the next track loads in the background. There is no disk access during playback |
| **Real time** | The thread that hands audio to the DAC asks rtkit for real-time priority (as PipeWire does), without root permissions; the signal path shows the priority obtained |
| **Multichannel** | Up to 7.1 without downmixing to stereo. If the device uses another channel order (ALSA: FL FR RL RR FC LFE…), the samples are reordered without touching their values; bit-perfect verified in 5.1 and 7.1 |
| **Test signals** | Settings › Audio › Test signals: tones generated on the fly that take the same path as the music and go back to what was playing when they end. *DAC sample rate*: 1 kHz at −20 dBFS at each PCM rate (44.1–384 kHz) and in DSD64/128/256 (with the in-house modulator), to read the rate on the DAC's display. *Speakers*: the tone on each channel in turn (stereo, 5.1, 7.1) in the file's order. *Bit-perfect (DTS)*: a DTS stream inside a CD-quality WAV (encoded with FFmpeg): an AV receiver plays the tone only if every bit arrives intact |
| **CUE** | CD images with a `.cue`: the library shows their tracks (title, performer, album, date, genre and ReplayGain from the sheet; format and cover from the file) instead of the whole file. Sheets in UTF-8 or Windows-1252; if the `.cue` names a `.wav` and the file is a `.flac`, it is found anyway. Each track ends exactly where the next one starts: with gapless playback, the disc plays complete, bit for bit |
| **HDCD** | Settings › Audio › HDCD decoding: HDCD CDs are detected in the first seconds of each 16-bit track and decoded to 20 bits with libhdcd (output identical to FFmpeg's HDCD filter); everything else stays bit-perfect |
| **Reference DSP** | All in double precision, with the signal path showing every stage: **FIR convolution** (filters from REW, Dirac, Acourate… mono, stereo or 4-channel true stereo, up to 2^20 taps, partitioned overlap-save with pocketfft; identical to direct convolution with ~10⁻¹⁵ error), **crossfeed** for headphones (Bauer/bs2b: default, Chu Moy, Jan Meier; identical to libbs2b), **upsampling** ×2/×4/the DAC's maximum with a **choice of filter** (linear phase, intermediate, minimum or steep linear; SoX VHQ), configurable **headroom** before the DSP with a **clipping detector**, an optional **true-peak limiter** at the end of processing (4× oversampled true peak, −1 dBTP ceiling by default, 1.5 ms look-ahead with a linear attack and 50 ms release, all channels together; bit-exact below the ceiling and never on the bit-perfect path), and TPDF or **F-weighted noise-shaped dither** (only on outputs of up to 20 bits; at 24 bits the flat −144 dBFS floor is already far below any DAC and shaping would only raise it by 18 dB). A track with more bits than the DAC (24 bits on a 16-bit one, 32 bits, lossy or converted DSD) is requantized with dither instead of being truncated. Float sources (MP3, AAC, Opus, Vorbis, float WAV, converted DSD) reach the DSP in double precision with anything above 0 dBFS intact, so volume, ReplayGain or headroom lower it instead of it being clipped in the decoder. Digital silence goes out as zeros (no dither cushion), and pause and seek fades return to the DAC's grid with dither. Volume, ReplayGain and headroom glide sample by sample across a block, with no clicks or *zipper* noise when moving the volume (at a gapless join the new gain still starts at the exact sample). Denormals are flushed on the audio thread (FTZ/DAZ): an EQ over digital silence does not become 50 times slower |
| **PCM → DSD** | Converts PCM to DSD64/128/256/512 with an 8th-order sigma-delta modulator (Schreier NTF with optimized zeros, H∞ = 1.5) and sends it as native DSD or DoP. DSP and digital volume are applied first. In-band signal-to-noise ratio (20 Hz–20 kHz, 997 Hz at −6.9 dBFS): ~113 dB at DSD64 (108 dB with the earlier 7th order) and well above 24 bits from DSD128 up; the loop is stable at full scale; gapless between tracks. Each channel is modulated on its own core: stereo DSD512 (22.6 MHz per channel) runs in real time |
| **Hardware volume** | Settings › Audio › Volume control › DAC (or Automatic): **with PipeWire, the sink volume** (the DAC's own control when the device has one: PipeWire moves the ALSA mixer and leaves the samples untouched), so the desktop's volume keys and Sonkore's slider move the same control and the path stays bit-perfect; on a virtual output (an effects chain) Sonkore warns that PipeWire scales in software. Sonkore follows the sink that really feeds the stream: if Easy Effects or another filter opens or closes, the signal path, the output list and the volume control change with it, and the music carries on through the next output (WirePlumber asks every player to pause when its sink disappears, meant for an unplugged DAC; Sonkore tells that case apart from a filter closing). With a direct output (Linux: `hw:`, the ALSA mixer in dB; Windows: WASAPI exclusive, the device's own volume control through `IAudioEndpointVolume` when it implements it in hardware), the volume moves the DAC's control and the engine stays at unity gain, so the audio stays bit-perfect at any volume. It follows changes made elsewhere (alsamixer, the Windows volume control, another app). If the DAC has no control of its own, digital volume is used and Sonkore says so |
| **Visualizer** | A live spectrum of what is playing (24 bands, 2048-point FFT, synchronized with the device's latency) next to the cover: bars with peaks that hold and fall, mirror, smooth line or LEDs, or off. It is drawn whole into an image with QPainter, as in foobar2000 or MusicBee: antialiasing, fractional heights (it moves by fractions of a pixel), every bar the same width on the monitor's pixel grid and a gradient by height; it looks the same with the software renderer as with OpenGL or Vulkan. It reads a copy of the output: it never touches the audio |
| **DSP** | Pitch in semitones with 7 decimals, tempo and rate with **SoundTouch** (WSOLA, very little CPU; advanced parameters: anti-aliasing filter, sequence, seek window and overlap). Values are kept between sessions without losing a bit of the `double`. If the device does not accept the track's sample rate, it is resampled with SoX VHQ (instead of miniaudio's linear resampler). Optional TPDF dither, digital volume (100 % = bit-perfect) with a scale linear in dB over the last 60 dB (1 dB steps with the wheel and `Ctrl+↑/↓`, the value in dB on hover) |
| **Themes** | 39 themes: Sonkore, Basalt, Verdant, Porcelain, Slate, Crimson, Graphite, Fjord, Nocturne, Parchment, Obsidian, Dusk, Classic (compact), Ocean, Forest, Ember, Mocha, Aurora, Neon, Sakura, Sand, Lavender, Glacier, Paper and Carbon (high contrast), Studio, Daylight, Ironwood, Islands, Polar, Velvet, Citrus, Solstice, Linen, Harvest, Metro, Cocoa, Harbor and Abyss; plus accent color, density and interface scale |
| **Desktop** | Its own icon (SVG, PNG, `.ico`). A tray icon with controls and close-to-tray (off by default), MPRIS (GNOME/KDE media widget, media keys, `playerctl`) and quick actions on the dock icon. On Windows, previous/play/next buttons on the taskbar thumbnail and progress on the button. Opening a file while Sonkore is already open hands it to the running instance |
| **Smoothness** | A render loop on its own thread by default (the full refresh rate: 144 Hz measured), a progress bar interpolated every frame, live seeking while dragging, an optional FPS counter and a renderer choice (OpenGL / Vulkan / Software, in Settings › General). With the software renderer, the rounded shapes Qt draws badly at fractional scales (circles, small radii and every rounded rectangle with a border: knobs, switches, checkboxes, window buttons, menus, settings cards, fields, format badges) are drawn with antialiasing on the monitor's pixels; checked at 125, 150, 166 and 175 %; and with the window maximized, the last rows and columns of pixels that renderer leaves unpainted at those scales are filled in (the desktop used to show through a line at the bottom) |
| **Adaptive interface** | Configurable wheel speed with smooth scrolling (the touchpad follows the finger 1:1). A sidebar resized by dragging its edge, with an adjustable split between folders and playlists (double click resets); in icon-only mode, the Folders view shows the subfolders for browsing. An integrated title bar in the theme's colors (moving and resizing are done by the compositor, as in a native app), with its outline and rounded corners even on all four sides at any scale, also with the software renderer (drawn with antialiasing on the pixels the window really shows, even when the compositor crops the last row at 125 %, 150 % or 166 %); optionally the system title bar (Adwaita style on GNOME, KWin's on Plasma). A minimum of 760×480 logical pixels (fits 1920×1080 at 200 %): the sidebar switches to icons only in narrow windows (or by choice), headers and settings rearrange, and the pitch and tempo window scrolls on short screens |
| **Memory** | Pages are created when opened, and Settings / Equalizer / Queue are released on leaving; the queue and playlists read tracks from the library without copying them; covers are decoded at their visible size and rounded while decoding (no GPU effects); only the graphics drivers for the GPU present are loaded, and compiled pipelines are kept between sessions; file dialogs (QtQuick.Dialogs, three libraries) load the first time one opens, and covers are decoded with their format already identified, without asking every image plugin. In the audio engine, the pitch and tempo processor, the resampler, the DSP buffers and the visualizer's copy exist only while in use (SoundTouch is released entirely when back to neutral). With PipeWire spoken natively, the PulseAudio and JACK clients are not loaded (libpulse pulls in libsndfile, FLAC, Vorbis, Opus, LAME and mpg123): they are created only if PipeWire fails or a JACK device is saved. The packages ship only the image plugins in use (SVG, JPEG, WebP, GIF). FFmpeg is not linked: it is opened only when scanning or playing a format that needs it, so a FLAC/WAV/MP3 session does not load its ~190 libraries (~4 MB private and ~29 MB of RSS). The library is read from JSON without an intermediate copy and shares repeated strings (artist, album, genre, codec): 3.4 MB instead of 9 MB after loading a ~2,600-track library, with half the peak at startup. With that same library: ~255 MB with OpenGL (automatic) or Vulkan (previously ~450–480 MB), ~150 MB with the software renderer. With the software renderer, Qt repaints the whole window every frame when the scale is not an integer (125 %, 150 %…); Sonkore asks it to repaint only what changes and goes back to full repaints while half the window moves (a scrolling list): playing with the visualizer in a 4K window, from ~30 % of a core to ~9 %. On Wayland, animations also advance at the pace of the compositor's frame callbacks, for smoother scrolling |
| **CPU** | The thread that decodes and processes sleeps for as long as the DAC takes to free space (about 40 wake-ups per second instead of 500), and the interface polls the engine 20 times per second only while playing (4 while paused). Measured on a 2.8 GHz Xeon, as a percentage of one core: 44.1/16 bit-perfect 1.1 %; EQ 1.5 %; linear-phase EQ 2.6 % (5.9 % at 96 kHz); 65,536-tap convolution 4.4 %; ×4 to 176.4 kHz 2.6 %; DSD64 → PCM 2.9 %; DSD256 → PCM 7 % |
| **Safe start** | If the app closes during startup, the next start steps the graphics settings down one level (chosen renderer → automatic → no render thread → software) and says so; if the GPU cannot start, it restarts on its own with a safer one. `Sonkore --safe-mode` uses the software renderer just that once. Logging out or `kill` save the queue and settings like a normal exit, and the output is never left hanging |

Shortcuts: `Space` play/pause · `Ctrl+←/→` previous/next · `Ctrl+↑/↓` volume · `Ctrl+F` search · `Ctrl+A` select all · `Enter` play the selection · `Del` remove from the playlist or queue · `Ctrl+E` equalizer · `Ctrl+,` settings · `Ctrl+Q` quit.

## First run

"System default" output (PipeWire on Linux), the software renderer (the lightest; Settings › General offers OpenGL and Vulkan), the equalizer off (Flat profile) and pitch and tempo off (neutral values): the bit-perfect path stays intact until you turn something on.

## Audio flow

```
                worker (not RT)                                                         RT callback
decoder s32 ─┬─ bypass ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬─▶ ring<int32> ─▶ miniaudio (s32)
             └─ s32→f32 ─▶ SoundTouch ─▶ f64 ─▶ SuperEQ ─▶ vol ─▶ [soxr] ──────────────┘
                (or s32→f64 directly if there is only EQ/volume)  ─▶ quantize (+TPDF)
```

* **Bit-perfect**: with neutral pitch and tempo, the EQ off or flat and the volume at 100 %, the decoded integers reach the driver untouched. The device is opened in `s32` at the file's sample rate, which changes automatically with each track.
* **Resampling**: if the device does not accept the file's rate (WASAPI shared, DACs without 44.1 kHz, DSD), it is opened at its own rate and the worker converts with SoX VHQ (linear phase). The status bar says so ("resampled from 44.1 kHz") and the bit-perfect indicator goes off.
* **Real time**: the callback only copies from a lock-free queue. The buffer is sized at ≥ 2× the device buffer and pre-filled before starting, so there are no underruns either when playing or when seeking.
* **Linux, direct output**: when playing through an `hw:` device (◆), Sonkore asks the sound server for the DAC with the standard device reservation protocol (`org.freedesktop.ReserveDevice1`), as professional audio programs do. PipeWire lets it go, other apps fall silent or move to another output, and when it is released (the **Release DAC** button, the speaker menu, a minute paused or closing Sonkore) PipeWire takes it back on its own. If another program that does not use that protocol holds the DAC, Sonkore says it is busy.

## User data

Everything goes into `sonkore` folders, with no company folder:

| What | Linux | Windows |
|---|---|---|
| Settings | `~/.config/sonkore/sonkore.conf` | registry `HKCU\Software\sonkore` |
| Library, playlists, session (queue and position), EQ profiles, AutoEQ index | `~/.local/share/sonkore/` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sonkore\` |
| Cache (graphics pipelines, covers for the system, downloaded covers) | `~/.cache/sonkore/` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\cache\sonkore\` |
