# Features

[← Back](../README.md)

| Area | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Formats** | FLAC (up to 24 bit / 384 kHz and beyond, 32-bit too), WAV and AIFF (PCM and float), MP3, ALAC and AAC (`.m4a`/`.mp4`), Vorbis and FLAC in Ogg, CAF and MKA/WebM, decoded with Symphonia; DSD (`.dsf`/`.dff`, compressed DST too) with Sonkore's own decoder; **Opus, Monkey's Audio (APE), WavPack, TTA, TAK, Musepack, WMA, Wave64, AC-3, E-AC-3 and DTS through FFmpeg** when it is installed (loaded at run time, FFmpeg 5 to 8: nothing to install with Sonkore, and without it those files are listed but say they need FFmpeg). Integer lossless formats (FLAC, ALAC, AIFF, WAV, APE, WavPack, TTA, TAK) reach the DAC unconverted, so they keep the bit-perfect path; in MP4 the AAC encoder padding is trimmed, and Opus's pre-skip and end padding too (gapless). CD images in any of these formats play track by track |
| **Library** | Watched folders with recursive, incremental scanning (inotify on Linux), a JSON cache (the same files earlier Sonkore versions wrote), tags, embedded covers or `cover.jpg`, and **missing covers from the Cover Art Archive** (the album is looked up on MusicBrainz by artist and album; the picture stays in the cache, never in your folders). Off by default: turn it on in Settings › Library. Track and album views, and the **folder tree in the sidebar**. The queue, the equalizer and the settings are on the playback bar |
| **Columns** | 20 columns (by default file name, format, time and favourite, in both the full view and the mini player; any can be removed; the first one takes the free space; title, artist, album, genre, year, track, disc, time, format, codec, sample rate, bits, channels, bitrate, size, file, folder, date added, modified…): shown or hidden with a right click on the header, **moved by dragging the header**, resized by dragging the divider (which follows the pointer; a double click restores the width), and sorted by clicking the header |
| **Selection and drag and drop** | Multiple selection with Ctrl / Shift / Ctrl+A. Tracks are dragged onto a playlist in the sidebar, the queue or "new playlist", and a playlist is reordered by dragging |
| **Gapless** | The next track is opened ahead of time and spliced without silence, sample-exact and without reopening the device, also with the equalizer, pitch or volume in use (verified bit for bit). Encoder padding is trimmed (LAME in MP3, AAC in MP4). If the sample rate or channels change, the DAC is reopened in the new format. The end of the last track is never cut off: the device stops only after it has played its buffer |
| **ReplayGain** | Per-track or per-album leveling (ReplayGain 2.0, −18 LUFS reference), with preamp and *true-peak* clipping prevention (the peak stays at −1 dBTP, the EBU R128 ceiling, because a 4× meter can read up to ~0.6 dB low). It reads `REPLAYGAIN_*` tags (FLAC, Ogg, MP4, ID3 `TXXX`); untagged tracks are measured in the background with ebur128 (EBU R128), album by album, with mono weighted as it is heard (on both speakers), 7.1 side channels counted and the *true peak* of lossy files measured above 0 dBFS. Files are never modified: measured values stay in the library cache. Off by default; the gain changes at the exact sample of a gapless join |
| **Playlists** | Create, rename, delete, add from any view, import/export M3U/M3U8. A queue with shuffle and repeat (all/one) that is reordered by dragging and restored on reopening. A click on the title in the playback bar takes the open list to the playing song (or opens its folder) |
| **Mini player** | `Ctrl+M`: the window turns into a small player (Winamp style) with its own list below: the queue or a library folder, with its own columns (moved by dragging their heads) and order. A search box that filters as you type (`Ctrl+F`; `Enter` plays the first result) and a button that takes the list to the playing song (switching to its folder if needed). When the first library folder is added, the list shows it on its own |
| **Settings per output** | Each output device remembers its equalizer, convolution, crossfeed, headroom, limiter, dither, oversampling and DSD settings: headphones and speakers each come back with their own when you switch outputs (Settings › Audio; a new output starts from the current settings) |
| **Equalizer** | 10-band graphic or parametric with up to 32 bands (peak, shelves, LP/HP, BP, notch), an interactive response curve (drag points in frequency and gain without crossing into neighbouring bands, wheel = Q, double click = standard frequency in graphic mode), preamp with a clipping warning and an "Auto" mode, 13 presets and your own profiles, import/export of parametric profiles (`.txt`, including Equalizer APO's slope-based shelves). Peak and shelf bands use a **matched design** (Vicanek) instead of the bilinear transform: the curve does not collapse near Nyquist (a +6 dB band at 16 kHz at 44.1 kHz gives +6.0 dB, previously +2.1) and sounds the same at 44.1, 96 or 192 kHz. In graphic mode the gains are solved together so the curve passes through each slider's value (one-octave bands overlap: ten at +6 dB used to give +8.8 dB). Turning the equalizer on, off or flat is a ~10 ms fade, with no jumps. Optional **linear phase**: the same curve as an FIR filter (within 0.01 dB of the biquads; from 0.5 s to 4 s of taps depending on how much the narrowest band rings, so a Q 10 notch in the bass reaches its full depth), which delays every frequency equally and leaves transient timing and the stereo image intact; it switches on, changes and switches off during playback without dropouts or repeats, also across gapless tracks |
| **AutoEQ** | Searches the ~7,000 headphones in [AutoEq](https://github.com/jaakkopasanen/AutoEq), applies the parametric correction and saves it as a profile |
| **Output** | Devices listed by backend: on Linux PipeWire first, **spoken natively** (with "System default"): PipeWire's graph follows each track's sample rate while Sonkore plays (*Follow the track's rate*), the stream volume stays at 100 %, and the signal path warns if PipeWire converts the rate or a software volume drops below 100 %. Then the cards as direct ALSA `hw:` (exclusive, bit-perfect; the DAC is asked from the sound server with the device reservation protocol). On Windows, WASAPI shared or **exclusive** (direct, bit-perfect). Switching devices happens live and keeps the position; on Linux, a DAC plugged in or out is noticed and said in the status line. After a rate change the DAC gets a moment of silence to lock before the music (Settings › Audio, 0–2 s). A direct output is given back to the system after a minute paused or stopped (*Give the DAC back when paused*, on by default) or at once with *Release DAC*; play takes it again where the track was. *Play from memory* (on by default) reads each file into RAM on a thread of its own (up to 2 GB), so playback never waits on the disk. **Test signals** (Settings › Audio): a 1 kHz tone at −20 dBFS at a chosen rate or as DSD64/128/256, and each speaker in turn, played through the same path as the music; what was playing comes back afterwards |
| **DSD** | Settings › Audio › DSD playback: *Convert to PCM* (the default, any DAC; with an **in-house double-precision converter**: a 160 dB FIR evaluated through byte tables up to 352.8 kHz and then rubato, DSD64 → 88.2 kHz flat to 30 kHz, DSD128 and up → 176.4 kHz flat to 50–70 kHz; time-aligned with the source and with DSD's ultrasonic noise filtered: ~−120 dBFS above the band versus ~−28 dBFS with FFmpeg's converter), *Native* (Linux: DSD_U32_BE/LE through ALSA to DACs that offer it on an `hw:` output; as high as the DAC goes) or *DoP* (DSD over PCM on a direct or exclusive output). DSF and DSDIFF, and **DST** too (compressed DSDIFF, the SACD format): an in-house DST decoder (ISO/IEC 14496-3, subpart 10) returns the exact bits frame by frame, so it also plays as native DSD or DoP; the bitstream arrives intact (verified byte by byte), with DSD silence (`0x69`) instead of zeros, a few ms of pre-roll so the DAC can switch modes, DoP markers always alternating, and gapless playback between DSD tracks of the same rate. DSP and digital volume do not apply to DSD; hardware volume does. If the output cannot carry it intact, it is converted to PCM |
| **Signal path** | The status pill in the playback bar ("Bit-perfect · 96 kHz", "Resampled · 48 kHz", "Processed · 44.1 kHz") opens it, and the **Now playing** page (click the cover in the bar) shows it next to the large cover and the queue: every stage from the file to the DAC (source, decoder, HDCD, DSD, pitch/tempo, equalizer, ReplayGain, volume, resampling, requantization and output) with its quality, as in Roon: *Lossless*, *High quality*, *Enhanced* (chosen DSP) or *Low quality* |
| **Multichannel** | Up to 7.1 without downmixing to stereo |
| **CUE** | CD images with a `.cue`: the library shows their tracks (title, performer, album, date, genre and ReplayGain from the sheet; format and cover from the file) instead of the whole file. Sheets in UTF-8 or Windows-1252; if the `.cue` names a `.wav` and the file is a `.flac`, it is found anyway. Each track ends exactly where the next one starts: with gapless playback, the disc plays complete, bit for bit |
| **HDCD** | Settings › Audio › HDCD decoding: HDCD CDs are detected in the first seconds of each 16-bit track and decoded to 20 bits with a port of libhdcd (sample for sample the same output); everything else stays bit-perfect |
| **Reference DSP** | All in double precision, with the signal path showing every stage: **FIR convolution** (filters from REW, Dirac, Acourate… mono, stereo or 4-channel true stereo, up to 2^20 taps, partitioned overlap-save with realfft; identical to direct convolution with ~10⁻¹⁵ error), **crossfeed** for headphones (Bauer/bs2b: default, Chu Moy, Jan Meier; identical to libbs2b), **upsampling** ×2/×4/the DAC's maximum with a **choice of filter** (linear phase or steep linear, with rubato; or minimum phase, without pre-ringing, and intermediate phase, a polyphase FIR of its own made minimum phase by the cepstral method, with the same ~190 dB stop band), configurable **headroom** before the DSP with a **clipping detector**, an optional **true-peak limiter** at the end of processing (4× oversampled true peak, −1 dBTP ceiling by default, 1.5 ms look-ahead with a linear attack and 50 ms release, all channels together; bit-exact below the ceiling and never on the bit-perfect path), and TPDF or **F-weighted noise-shaped dither** (only on outputs of up to 20 bits; at 24 bits the flat −144 dBFS floor is already far below any DAC and shaping would only raise it by 18 dB). A track with more bits than the DAC (24 bits on a 16-bit one, 32 bits, lossy or converted DSD) is requantized with dither instead of being truncated. Float sources (MP3, AAC, Opus, Vorbis, float WAV, converted DSD) reach the DSP in double precision with anything above 0 dBFS intact, so volume, ReplayGain or headroom lower it instead of it being clipped in the decoder. Digital silence goes out as zeros (no dither cushion), and pause and seek fades return to the DAC's grid with dither. Volume, ReplayGain and headroom glide sample by sample across a block, with no clicks or *zipper* noise when moving the volume (at a gapless join the new gain still starts at the exact sample). |
| **Hardware volume** | Settings › Audio › Volume control › DAC (or Automatic): **with PipeWire, the sink volume** (the DAC's own control when the device has one: PipeWire moves the ALSA mixer and leaves the samples untouched), so the desktop's volume keys and Sonkore's slider move the same control and the path stays bit-perfect. With a direct output (Linux: `hw:`, the ALSA mixer in dB; Windows: WASAPI exclusive, the device's own volume control through `IAudioEndpointVolume`), the volume moves the DAC's control and the engine stays at unity gain, so the audio stays bit-perfect at any volume. If the DAC has no control of its own, digital volume is used and Sonkore says so |
| **Visualizer** | A live spectrum of what is playing (24 bands, 2048-point FFT, synchronized with the device's latency) next to the cover: bars with peaks that hold and fall, mirror, smooth line or LEDs, or off. It is drawn whole into an image (tiny-skia), as in foobar2000 or MusicBee: antialiasing, fractional heights (it moves by fractions of a pixel), every bar the same width on the monitor's pixel grid and a gradient by height. It reads a copy of the output: it never touches the audio |
| **DSP** | Pitch in semitones with 7 decimals, tempo and rate with **SoundTouch** (ported to Rust; WSOLA, very little CPU; advanced parameters: anti-aliasing filter, sequence, seek window and overlap). Values are kept between sessions without losing a bit of the `double`. If the device does not accept the track's sample rate, it is resampled with rubato. Optional TPDF dither, digital volume (100 % = bit-perfect) with a scale linear in dB over the last 60 dB (1 dB steps with the wheel and `Ctrl+↑/↓`, the value in dB on hover) |
| **Themes** | 39 themes: Sonkore, Basalt, Verdant, Porcelain, Slate, Crimson, Graphite, Fjord, Nocturne, Parchment, Obsidian, Dusk, Classic (compact), Ocean, Forest, Ember, Mocha, Aurora, Neon, Sakura, Sand, Lavender, Glacier, Paper and Carbon (high contrast), Studio, Daylight, Ironwood, Islands, Polar, Velvet, Citrus, Solstice, Linen, Harvest, Metro, Cocoa, Harbor and Abyss; plus accent color, density and interface scale. **Eight bundled fonts** (Inter, Manrope, Plus Jakarta Sans, Figtree, Outfit, DM Sans, Nunito Sans, Rubik) or the system's, applied at once to every window; the interface scale and the language take effect after a restart (Settings › Restart now, which keeps the session) |
| **Desktop** | Its own icon (SVG, PNG, `.ico`). A tray icon with controls and close-to-tray (off by default) and quick actions on the dock icon. On Linux, MPRIS (GNOME/KDE media widget, media keys, `playerctl`); on Windows, the system's media controls. Opening a file while Sonkore is already open hands it to the running instance |
| **Smoothness** | Drawn by Slint's software renderer, which repaints only what changes: the visualizer, the playing folder's bars and the position advance together on one 33 ms timer. Live seeking while dragging. Borders are drawn on whole screen pixels at fractional scales (125 %, 150 %, 175 %…) |
| **Adaptive interface** | Configurable wheel speed with smooth scrolling (the touchpad follows the finger 1:1). A sidebar resized by dragging its edge, with an adjustable split between folders and playlists (double click resets); in icon-only mode, the Folders view shows the subfolders for browsing. An integrated title bar in the theme's colors (moving and resizing are done by the compositor, as in a native app), with its outline and rounded corners at any scale. In narrow windows the sidebar switches to icons only in narrow windows (or by choice), headers and settings rearrange, and the pitch and tempo window scrolls on short screens |
| **Memory** | Pages are created when opened; the queue and playlists read tracks from the library without copying them; covers are decoded at their visible size. In the audio engine, the pitch and tempo processor, the resampler and the DSP buffers exist only while in use. PipeWire is spoken natively (loaded from the system), with no PulseAudio or JACK client |
| **CPU** | The thread that decodes and processes sleeps for as long as the DAC takes to free space, and the interface only redraws what moves. Playing with the visualizer: about 4.7 % of one core; paused: 0 % |

Shortcuts: `Space` play/pause · `Ctrl+←/→` previous/next · `Ctrl+↑/↓` volume · `Ctrl+F` search · `Ctrl+A` select all · `Enter` play the selection · `Del` remove from the playlist or queue · `Ctrl+E` equalizer · `Ctrl+,` settings · `Ctrl+Q` quit.

## First run

"System default" output (PipeWire on Linux), the equalizer off (Flat profile) and pitch and tempo off (neutral values): the bit-perfect path stays intact until you turn something on.

## Audio flow

```
            engine thread                                                              device
decoder ─┬─ bypass (integers untouched) ──────────────────────────────────────────┬─▶ ALSA hw: / PipeWire / WASAPI
         └─ f64 ─▶ SoundTouch ─▶ EQ ─▶ convolution ─▶ crossfeed ─▶ gain ─▶ resampler ─▶ limiter ─▶ quantize (+dither)
```

* **Bit-perfect**: with neutral pitch and tempo, the EQ off or flat and the volume at 100 %, the decoded integers reach the device untouched. The device is opened at the file's sample rate, which changes automatically with each track.
* **Resampling**: if the device does not accept the file's rate (WASAPI shared, DACs without 44.1 kHz, DSD), it is opened at its own rate and the engine converts with rubato. The signal path says so and the bit-perfect pill changes.
* **Linux, direct output**: when playing through an `hw:` device (◆), Sonkore asks the sound server for the DAC with the standard device reservation protocol (`org.freedesktop.ReserveDevice1`), as professional audio programs do.

## User data

Everything goes into `sonkore` folders, with no company folder:

| What | Linux | Windows |
|---|---|---|
| Settings | `~/.config/sonkore/sonkore.conf` | `%APPDATA%\sonkore\sonkore.conf` |
| Library, playlists, session (queue and position), EQ profiles, AutoEQ index | `~/.local/share/sonkore/` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sonkore\` |
| Cache (covers for the system, downloaded covers) | `~/.cache/sonkore/` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\cache\sonkore\` |
